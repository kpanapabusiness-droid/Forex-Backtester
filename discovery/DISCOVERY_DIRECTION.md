### M1 — Driver-shock-CONDITIONAL cross-timeframe residual (the freshest prior) · `explore-now`

- **Corpus citation.** Arc 3005 killed the cross-rate triangulation residual but **only unconditionally
  and only at H4** (fwd-convergence corr ≈ 0.01). Single-pair MTF is closed (TF-invariant) — but a
  cross-instrument lead-lag linked by the triangular identity was never tested.
- **Because.** `EURJPY ≡ EURUSD × USDJPY` is an identity. A large directional shock in a **driver leg**
  (e.g. EURUSD on the D1 close) *forces* the dependent cross to move; if the JPY market is in a quiet
  session, the quoted cross may re-price with a lag at the next H4 bar even though the *same-bar* H4
  residual is ≈ 0 on average. You are not forecasting price — you are riding a transient identity
  dislocation created by asynchronous re-pricing.
- **Falsifiable prediction.** Condition on `|EURUSD D1 return| > 1.5·ATR`; measure the **next-H4-bar**
  EURJPY residual (the part not explained by contemporaneous USDJPY). If real: `corr(driver_shock,
  next_bar_cross_residual) > 0.05` — materially above arc 3005's unconditional ≈ 0.01. **Falsifier:** if
  conditioning does not lift it above ~0.05, the cross re-prices within the bar (efficient) → dead.
- **Expression.** **One leg** — trade the cross; the driver leg is a *signal*, not a position. Cost ~0.8 bp
  (cross spread). No netted engine.
- **Most likely death.** Triangular arb is the most-policed FX relationship and H4 is slow; the honest
  prior is FAIL. **And** the conditioning produces a thin event set, so OOS-fold discipline must be
  ruthless (cf. the shock-continuation epoch-dependence death, arc 3019). Run the cheap conditional-corr
  observation FIRST; only build the leg if it clears ~0.05.

### O1 — Inelasticity-STATE conditioning of the surviving edges (build the latent variable ONCE) · `explore-now`

- **Corpus citation.** The 4 survivors (gap-fill, me_long, me_short, fbr) are mean-positive but **too thin
  to resolve folds** (arcs 2016/2017/2019/1023). The only surviving mechanism is *forced flow*; the only
  surviving calendar driver is *month-end* (arc 3015). The bid-ask spread series exists in the panel but
  has only ever been used as a **cost**, never as a **signal**.
- **Because.** Forced flows are not Poisson — they pile onto a handful of windows (month-end, quarter-end,
  fiscal-year-end, fixings) and onto bars where the book is **inelastic** (a flow moves price more per unit
  when depth is thin; the subsequent reversion is then larger). A wide bid-ask spread at the dislocation
  bar is a lag-free proxy for "how inelastic was the book." **The survivors infer inelasticity only from
  the calendar; the spread (and a forced-flow "density" score) are orthogonal proxies of the same latent
  state.** Concentrating risk into high-inelasticity bars raises edge-per-trade without adding coin-flip
  trades — a direct attack on the fold-resolution problem.
- **Falsifiable prediction.** Define a single **inelasticity-state** variable (proxies: forced-flow
  calendar-density score; entry-bar spread-z; range-compression-then-expansion). Within the existing
  fbr / month-end edges, per-trade realized R should rise **monotonically** with the state variable, and a
  book restricted to its top decile should have a **higher worst-fold ROI** than the unconditioned edge.
- **Expression.** A mask over existing edges + the existing combiner. No new data.
- **Collinearity note (load-bearing).** Forced-flow-density, spread-z, and "shallow the fbr/month-end
  trigger" are **the same variable in three costumes** — build the state variable ONCE with multiple
  proxies; do not spend three arcs on it.
- **Most likely death.** (1) Smaller sample → *worse* fold resolution if edge-concentration isn't steep
  enough (trading one thinness problem for another). (2) **Cost trap on the spread-z proxy:** wide-spread
  bars are exactly where you pay the most — any test MUST charge the *realized wide spread* at entry, or it
  is an Arc-10 cousin (fabricated optimism). (3) fbr specifically **does not thicken** (arc 1025: trigger
  depth and edge strength are coupled) — so the *shallowing* proxy is the weakest; expect it to confirm
  1025. The calendar-density and honestly-costed spread-z proxies are the live ones.

### L1 — Triangulation residual SECOND MOMENT (OU amplitude) at finer resolution · `explore-now`

- **Corpus citation.** Arc 3005 killed the residual **level** (median ≈ 0, fwd-convergence corr ≈ 0.01)
  but reported a non-trivial **residual std 1.23–1.58 bp** and |residual| > spread on 1.3–6.1% of H4 bars
  — and explicitly flagged M1/sub-H4 as **"out of apparatus scope, a different cost regime."** That flag
  is the untested door. The residual's *variance* was never traded.
- **Because.** Triangular arb pins the residual *mean* to ≈ 0, but the residual is the difference of three
  asynchronously-updating quotes; its **amplitude** spikes when one leg is temporarily stale (session
  handovers, one-currency news, fix windows). The residual behaves as a mean-reverting OU process around
  0 — you harvest the *amplitude* (a convergence trade), not the direction.
- **Falsifiable prediction.** Fit `dr = −θ·r·dt + σ·dW` to the residual at H4 → H1 → M15 → M1. The
  mean-reversion **half-life should shorten** as resolution rises; find the resolution where half-life
  < ~5 bars AND the OU amplitude σ exceeds the single-cross cost on > ~5% of bars. Trade: `|residual_z| > 2`
  → take the convergence (cheap) side, exit on convergence. **Falsifier:** if at *every* resolution down
  to M1 the fraction of bars where amplitude > capture-cost stays below ~5%, the triangulation thread is
  *fully* closed (level AND variance).
- **Expression.** **One leg** — trade the single quoted cross when it's cheap vs its synthetic reference
  (the synthetic is a reference, not a held position). No netted engine. Needs M1→H1 residual computation
  (the M1 bid/ask data exists).
- **Most likely death.** Knife-edge: ~1.3 bp amplitude vs ~1 bp cost is inside the noise band, and going
  to H1/M1 walks straight into the **H1 cost wall (~2× H4)** that killed the whole microstructure cluster
  (gotobi / round-number / WMR-fix / session-break). Also: a one-leg expression reintroduces directional
  exposure between entry and convergence. Expect cost to eat it — but the variance dimension is genuinely
  virgin, so a cheap multi-resolution observation is worth it to close the door cleanly.

### S1 — Take-the-loss-TAX entry geometry (a MODIFIER on M1/O1, not a standalone) · `explore-now`

- **Corpus citation.** The CI-locked invariant: a stop breach at/before the +1R partial books −1R;
  same-bar = SL-first; ambiguity never wins. fbr (arc 1013) is *literally* an instance of "reclaim, then
  enter, stop beyond the swept extreme" — favorable-move-first geometry — and it is the corpus's cleanest
  edge. The principle was never generalized.
- **Because.** The −1R tax punishes paths that touch the stop *before* the +1R partial. Entering a
  forced-flow reversion **after the reclaim prints, with the stop beyond the dislocation extreme**, makes
  the path "favorable-first," so the tax falls preferentially on the *coin-flip noise* trades you don't
  want — the engine's honest pessimism becomes a free path-quality filter.
- **Falsifiable prediction.** Partition entries by timing relative to the dislocation extreme
  (enter-on-touch vs enter-on-reclaim, stop-beyond-extreme). The reclaim geometry should reach
  +1R-partial-before-stop on a **higher fraction** of trades, by a margin **larger** than the difference
  in their stop-agnostic edge (i.e. the booked − theoretical gap shrinks).
- **Most likely death.** Probably **tautological** — fbr already exists *because* this works; applying it
  to fixings (which have no single "extreme" to place a stop beyond) may not generalize. Also risks
  **same-bar reclaim lookahead** (the reclaim must be detected without using the entry bar's future). Use
  as a refinement multiplier on M1/O1 survivors, not as its own arc.

### Q1 — Central-bank PEG / boundary-defense persistence (the long-shot; a NEW forced actor) · `explore-now` to detect

- **Corpus citation.** The 4 survivors are all *reversions of an over-extension*; the divergent seat asked

- **Falsifiable prediction.** Detect "boundary regimes" from OHLC alone (rolling realized vol below its

  regimes shows high win-rate / low-variance positive R; *outside* them the same rule is a coin-flip. The
  edge appears/disappears **with the regime**, not smoothly.
- **Most likely death.** (1) **Epoch-dependent** (cf. shock-continuation, arc 3019) — too few defended-band
  pair-years in 2010–2021+ to populate every calendar-year fold → fails the per-year gate by *absence of

## 0. The run-2 headline — Frontier A is closed by a CONSERVATION LAW, not by under-search

Five generative lenses ran independently; four of five reached the *same* closing argument, and the
adversarial cross-examination upheld it:

> **In a price-only charter, `frequency × per-trade-edge ≈ constant`.** The only continuously-tradeable
> state the apparatus samples is price, and price is a near-martingale — the honest ~0.49 coin-flip (proven
> instrument-, timeframe-, and metric-invariant, *and with the stop removed*, arc 3004) **is** that
> martingale property measured net of cost. Edge can therefore only live in **departures** from the
> martingale, and departures are by construction **rare and discrete** (forced flow: weekend gaps,
> month-end fixes, stop-sweep reclaims). To trade *more often* you must trade where the martingale holds →
> coin-flip. To raise per-trade edge on the rare events you need more capital-at-rest per event, not more
> events. **Hence no in-charter class can reach fundable frequency at non-zero expectancy.** The
> ~14–20× gap is the conservation law restated, not a search gap.

This is stronger than run 1's "the frontier is thin and conditional." It is: **the frontier is thin
*because* it must be.** The corpus is the proof, not an anecdote — across ~140 honest-era arcs *no
high-frequency net-positive class has ever appeared*, and the one obvious in-charter frequency lever
(stacking decorrelated legs) is analytically dead (arc 3021: at empirical leg-correlation ρ≈0.12,
P(all-folds-positive) plateaus ~0.33 and never reaches 0.9 at any leg count, because the legs share one
risk-off factor — see §2). **What breaks the law is exactly one thing: a continuous NON-PRICE state that
is itself non-martingale at the actionable sampling interval** (signed order-flow, rate differentials,
IV term-structure). That is not in-charter. It is the whole content of [`NEEDS_ENABLEMENT.md`](./NEEDS_ENABLEMENT.md)
(Frontier B), re-ranked this run around precisely this criterion.

**Guard against over-claiming (kept live from the arc-3004 council):** "the apparatus is incapable" is a
*seductive, search-ending* conclusion. The law above is falsifiable — it predicts that any newly-proposed
in-charter class will be *either* a coin-flip (continuous) *or* a thin rare-event leg (discrete), never

if either turns out fundable-frequency, the law is wrong and that is the highest-value outcome of the run.
They are *not* offered as a fundability route — by the law, they cannot be one.
